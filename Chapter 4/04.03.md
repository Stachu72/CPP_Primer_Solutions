# Exercise 4.3
> Order of evaluation for most of the binary operators is left undefined to give the compiler opportunities for optimization. This strategy presents a trade-off between efficient code generation and potential pitfalls in the use of the language by the programmer. Do you consider that an acceptable trade-off? Why or why not?

## Solution

After reading Lesson 4.1 **Fundamentals** (p. 134), I had to slightly modify my previous solutions due to the order of evaluation. My previous expression looked like this: 
```c++
cout << "The sum of elements: " << index << " and " << ++index << " is: " << *i + *(i+1) << endl;
```
The pitfall here is `++index`, which may be evaluated before the result is printed. What could give us (for example): 
```c++
The sum of elements: 1 and 1 is: 0 + 10 
```
Instead of:
```c++
The sum of elements: 0 and 1 is: 0 + 10
```

I think that if we will follow the rule: _If you change the value of an operand, don’t use that operand elsewhere in the
same expression._ everything should be fine. <br>

- _**Do you consider that an acceptable trade-off?**_ <br>
In my opinion, this might be confusing, but **C++** aims for efficiency. We have to accept these rules, not others.
